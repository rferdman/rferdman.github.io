User Guide
Kepler's Laws Interactive Explorer

This tool lets you explore all three of Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion interactively. Use the Law I / Law II / Law III tabs at the top right to switch between laws.

Each law has the same general layout: the orbit animation fills the main panel, with a sidebar on the right for the law description and controls. Below the animation are a live measurements bar, any graphs, and the playback controls. The planet always moves according to the real physics — Kepler's equations are solved numerically at every frame.

What it shows. Every planet orbits the Sun in an ellipse with the Sun at one focus. The r₁ and r₂ lines show the distances from the planet to each focus, and their sum always equals 2a — no matter where the planet is.

Controls bar (below the animation)
  • Semi-major axis a — drag to scale the orbit from 0.3 to 20 AU
  • Eccentricity e — drag from 0 (circular) toward 0.95 (very elongated)
  • Show orbit / Show foci — toggles the dashed orbit path, focus markers, r₁/r₂ lines
  • Pause / Play / Reset / Speed — control the animation
Planet presets (sidebar)

Click any planet pill to overlay its real orbital parameters. Multiple planets can be selected at once — each orbit is drawn in its own colour. Click an already-selected planet to make it active (its measurements are shown). Click active again to deselect. Clear all returns to manual sliders.

Dragging the planet

Hover over the planet — the cursor changes to a grab hand. Click and drag to move it anywhere around the orbit. The measurements bar updates live as you drag.

Measurements bar
r₁Distance from planet to F₁ (the Sun)
r₂Distance from planet to F₂ (the empty focus)
r₁ + r₂ = 2aAlways constant — Kepler's first law
eEccentricity of the active orbit
rₚPerihelion distance = a(1 − e)
rₐAphelion distance = a(1 + e)

What it shows. A line from the Sun to the planet sweeps equal areas in equal times. The blue sector (A₁, near perihelion) and gold sector (A₂, near aphelion) have the same area because the same time elapsed — even though they look very different in shape.

Planet presets

Click any planet pill to load its real eccentricity. The orbit, planet graphic, and speed/distance graph all update. Moving the eccentricity slider manually clears any preset.

Sweeping A₃

Click anywhere on the canvas to start an animated sweep (green, A₃) from the planet's current position. It stops automatically once the area equals A₁ and A₂. Click Clear A₃ in the measurements bar to reset.

Speed/distance graph

Shows orbital speed (green) and distance from the Sun (blue) across the full orbit, with phase 0 at perihelion. A moving playhead tracks the current position. With a preset active, axes show real km/s and AU.

What it shows. T² = a³ (in AU and years) for every planet, so T²/a³ ≈ 1.00 universally. The animation shows all five inner planets orbiting at their correct relative periods.

Log-log plot

Shows log T vs log a for all eight planets. Because T² = a³, the points lie exactly on a straight line of slope 3/2. Click any planet dot to show its a (AU) and T (years). Click again to dismiss.

Custom orbit

Use the Custom Orbit sliders in the sidebar to define any orbit from 0.1–32 AU. A dashed pink orbit and planet appear on the canvas; a pink diamond on the log-log plot always sits exactly on the T² = a³ line, however you adjust a.

Halley's Comet (Laws I & II)

The most dramatic preset — e = 0.967. The comet blazes through perihelion and barely crawls at aphelion. The tail always points away from the Sun and grows much longer near perihelion.

Verifying r₁ + r₂ = 2a (Law I)

Drag the planet around the orbit and watch the r₁ + r₂ value in the measurements bar — it stays exactly equal to 2a the whole way round.

Checking equal areas (Law II)

Pause near perihelion and click to sweep A₃. Then pause near aphelion and sweep again. The two green sectors will look very different in shape but always cover the same area.

The T² = a³ line (Law III)

Use the Custom Orbit sliders at different values of a. The diamond always sits exactly on the dashed reference line — Kepler's third law holds universally.
