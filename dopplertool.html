What is the Doppler Effect?

When a source of waves moves relative to an observer, the observed frequency differs from the emitted frequency. Motion toward the observer compresses the wavefronts (blueshift), while motion away stretches them (redshift).

The Doppler Formula

For a moving source (observer at rest):

f_obs = f_src × v_wave / (v_wave ∓ v_src)

where − applies when source approaches and + when it recedes. For light (non-relativistic):

Δλ/λ = v_r / c

Positive Δλ/λ = redshift; negative = blueshift.

Manual Mode
  • Drag the ★ Source and ◆ Observer freely anywhere in the field.
  • Use Play/Pause to start and stop wave emission.
  • Adjust emission frequency and wave speed with the sliders.
Auto-Motion Mode
  • The source starts at the left edge and moves steadily toward the observer.
  • The observer is locked at the right-centre of the field.
  • Use the Source Speed slider to control how fast it moves.
  • Watch the wavefronts compress ahead of the source (blueshift) and stretch behind it (redshift). After passing the observer the shift flips sign.
  • Reset returns the source to the starting position.
Astronomical Context

The Doppler effect is fundamental in astrophysics — used to measure radial velocities of stars and galaxies, detect exoplanets, and discover binary systems. Redshifted spectra from distant galaxies provided the first evidence for the expansion of the Universe.
